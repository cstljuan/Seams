## Narration (optional, about 190 words)

1. (0–8) Utilities plan their projects separately. Where two plans come close, who is checking?
2. (8–20) Seams checks two things for each pair across utilities. How close they are, which sets a tier. And whether their in-service dates fall within a year of each other. Start dates are not in the data yet, so that is our stand-in.
3. (20–52) This is the live app. Ten sample projects from the Sperry Tech package, six cross-utility pairs, ranked by distance. Turn on time overlaps only. Pair two: Georgia Power's McIntosh to Purrysburg reactors, and Dominion's Jasper to Okatie line. About nine kilometres apart, tier four, in service a hundred and fifty-two days apart. Every number says how accurate it is and where it came from.
4. (52–68) For this pair, the model puts the cost of not coordinating at about seventeen to thirty thousand dollars. That is a P10 to P90 range from a seeded simulation over public benchmarks. An estimate, not a quote.
5. (68–81) What is real today: the sample data, a tested engine for distance, tier, timing and cost, MongoDB Atlas storage, and a live app. Not yet: full filings, start dates and exact line routes.
6. (81–90) Seams. Where planned grid projects meet.
